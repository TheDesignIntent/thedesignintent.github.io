Latitude 9000 Design Development

Latitude 9000 Ultra Premium Line of commercial notebooks.
Role, Design Director for this line of ultra premium products.
Latitude 9000 wasn't about speeds and feeds. It was about a specific customer. The executive who really wants an Apple computer and has to buy one of ours due to company IT policies. That reframe changed everything about how we approached the ultra-premium tier, away from IT talking points and toward the emotional and experiential logic that moves a premium buyer. My team developed the 5 Ws as our strategic foundation. Why, Where, What, Who, When. We used it to build a CMF and design direction grounded in trend research, premium customer journey mapping, and material exploration that asked genuine questions: do we use lightweight magnesium or machined aluminum? Can carbon hybrid carry the premium signal we need? The 9000 was always meant to be a test bed for new materials, new features, new processes, new forms introduced here before scaling to higher volume lines. 9000 also established was the aesthetic DNA for the entire Latitude generation 14 family. The design decisions made at the ultra premium tier. The material language, proportion, surface treatment, CMF direction would cascade down to the 7000, 5000, and 3000. That's the architecture that most portfolios don't show, the relationship between where design takes risks and where it operationalizes them at scale.

CMF strategy for the entire line of Latitude.

The ultra-premium positioning didn't sustain commercially. Dell's sales infrastructure is built for volume, and a tier that sells ~ 100,000 units a year with a premium story that's hard to articulate to a sales rep is a difficult thing to hold in a large organization. I agreed with the decision to move away from it, not because the design was wrong, but because the market thesis was genuinely difficult to own at scale. What I learned from that outcome is that design can establish a tier and build the product to deserve it, but the organization has to be able to sell it and articulate what they want. When those things aren't aligned, the design work survives in the products downstream. The 9000's aesthetic lived on in the 7000, 5000, and 3000.
