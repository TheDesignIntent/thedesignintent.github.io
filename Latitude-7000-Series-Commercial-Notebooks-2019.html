Latitude 7000 Series Commercial Notebooks 2019

I led the design of the Dell Latitude 7000 series as Design Lead for the full commercial notebook family. This grew to a ten-system program spanning two product generations and three distinct material platforms. The program started as eight systems and grew to ten mid-cycle, which meant extending a design language that was already in production without fracturing the family DNA we had established. Managing that expansion while keeping three platforms coherent. Woven carbon fiber at the premium tier, CNC-machined aluminum for standard enterprise, and magnesium alloy for the ultra-portable line. This required a level of systems thinking that goes beyond visual consistency. Each material has its own tolerance stack, its own surface finishing logic, its own constraints on radii and transitions. Holding a unified design language across all three simultaneously, across two generations, meant every decision had downstream consequences I had to track and own. This was a very complex system to design.
My role was embedded in engineering from the beginning, not introduced at program gates. I maintained design authority through the full ID to ME translation, participated in proto build reviews, and served as the design resolution point for DFM issues as they surfaced from manufacturing and supplier feedback. DFM issues don't arrive as clean questions with obvious answers. They arrive as cost or schedule pressure, and my job was to evaluate what a proposed change actually cost in design intent, then drive a resolution that the planning team accepted, and engineering and manufacturing could execute. I traveled to manufacturing and supplier facilities in Asia for detail design and production validation. CMF verification at production scale, tooling reviews, surface quality confirmation, and watching assembly processes firsthand. You cannot make good DFM trade off calls from a conference room. Seeing how a part comes off a tool, how an operator handles a lid assembly, changes what you're willing to defend and what you let go.
The program also required me to operate well outside the design studio. I presented design work in cross functional executive reviews that included engineering leadership, planning executives, and procurement executives. Rooms where the conversation is denominated in unit cost and program timeline, not design rationale. These aren't design friendly environments. Defending a material choice or a surface treatment in front of procurement means translating design intent into business terms clearly enough that the people holding the cost model understand what they're trading away if they override it. On the marketing side, I co-developed the product story and positioning with the marketing team, shaping how the three platform material strategy was communicated to enterprise IT buyers. I also participated in Dell's Customer Advisory Councils, bringing enterprise IT procurement decision makers into the design studio for hands on product engagement and representing design thinking in a room full of people who buy at scale and care deeply about total cost of ownership and fleet consistency.
The Latitude 7000 program is the benchmark I use when I think about what program leadership actually means. It was the first time I was responsible for a family of that size across its full arc, from concept decisions that locked in tooling investment to production release on multiple simultaneous platforms and it set the standard for how I think about scope, accountability, and what it takes to hold design quality across a complex, multi-stakeholder program.

Design Story and Development

Narrow borders and a new hinge allow for a bigger display, providing users with an amazing front of screen experience. The display also features a next-generation privacy panel that protects data from prying eyes, particularly useful for business travelers.

Meticulously crafted from high quality materials for today’s commercial customers. The new 2-in-1s and laptops are available in Titan Gray machined aluminum or Mineral Gray lightweight carbon fiber finish. The aluminum option is expertly engineered with a trio of finishes [hairline brushed, bead blasted and diamond cut], complete with tone-on-tone soft-touch paint to offer an outstanding tactile experience.

The ultra-quiet, modern keyboard minimizes fatigue and elevates the users’ typing experience. The design team streamlined the design to incorporate the power button fingerprint reader into the keyboard. On the click pad, the design team used a new material that feels like glass, but has the toughness and lightness of polymer.
Both the power button fingerprint reader and Dell Express Sign-in serve as added layers of protection, as well as speeding time to log-in. Not only does the facial recognition authentication enable faster time to productivity, it also reduces power consumption by locking the system when the user walks away. In addition to Windows Hello, the Latitude Series offers Federal and enterprise-class security options.

Many many parts to check and qualify prior to shipping.
